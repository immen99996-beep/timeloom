Countries Where the Weekend Is Not Saturday and Sunday

Updated Sep 27, 2026

Most of the world takes Saturday and Sunday off, but not all of it. In much of the Middle East and North Africa the weekend is Friday and Saturday, and a few countries have a one-day weekend or a different pair of days. If you work with teams there, your shared working week is shorter than you might expect.

Some of these countries have changed in recent years. Saudi Arabia moved from a Thursday–Friday weekend to Friday–Saturday in 2013. The United Arab Emirates moved from Friday–Saturday to Saturday–Sunday in 2022, which is why it does not appear in the table. Private companies sometimes follow different days from the government, so ask your contacts which days they work.

How many days do you actually share?

A team in Riyadh works Sunday to Thursday. A team in Berlin works Monday to Friday. They share four days: Monday to Thursday. Sunday is a working day only in Riyadh, and Friday only in Berlin.

With the time difference added, the shared window can get small. On a Thursday, the last working day in Riyadh, an afternoon meeting in Berlin may already be after hours in Riyadh, which is one or two hours ahead depending on the season.

Practical tips

  • Put recurring meetings on Monday to Wednesday. They are working days for both weekend patterns and are far from either weekend.
  • Avoid Friday deadlines for teams that start their weekend on Friday, and Sunday deadlines for everyone else.
  • In Indonesia and Malaysia, Friday is a working day, but many offices take a longer midday break for Friday prayers. Avoid Friday lunchtime there.
